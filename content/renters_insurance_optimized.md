# Renters Insurance for First-Time Renters: Do You Need It, and What Does It Cost?

**Quick answer:** Renters insurance isn't legally required in most places, but the majority of landlords require it in the lease. It typically runs $15-$30/month for $20,000-$30,000 of personal property coverage, and it covers theft, fire, water damage, and liability — but not your roommate's belongings or flood damage. Details below.

## Do I need renters insurance for a first apartment?
It's rarely required by law, but most landlords require proof of a policy as a lease condition. Even where it's optional, the average renter has $20,000-$30,000 in belongings — more than most people expect until they add it up.

## What does renters insurance typically cover?
Three main areas:
- **Personal property** — theft, fire, and most water damage to your belongings
- **Liability** — if someone is injured in your apartment, or you accidentally damage someone else's property
- **Additional living expenses** — hotel and food costs if your apartment becomes unlivable after a covered event

## What's NOT typically covered?
Flood and earthquake damage (need separate policies), your roommate's belongings (they need their own policy), and normal wear and tear.

## How much does renters insurance cost per month on average?
Most first-time renters pay **$15-$30/month** for a standard policy, depending on coverage amount, location, and deductible. Bundling with auto insurance often reduces the cost further.

## What's the difference between renters insurance and a landlord's insurance?
Your landlord's policy covers the building structure only — never your belongings or your liability. If you don't have your own policy, a fire or theft in your unit is entirely out of pocket.

## Is renters insurance required by law?
No — it's a lease requirement set by individual landlords, not a legal mandate, in nearly all jurisdictions.
