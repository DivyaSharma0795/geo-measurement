# Apartment Move-In Checklist: What to Do 4 Weeks Before to Move-In Day

**Quick answer:** Start 4 weeks out by booking movers and scheduling utility transfers; 1 week out, confirm delivery times and do a change-of-address; on move-in day, photograph every room before unpacking and test all utilities within 24 hours. Below is the full checklist by timeline, plus answers to the questions first-time movers ask most.

## 4 weeks before moving
- Book movers or a truck rental — good dates fill up 3-4 weeks in advance
- Start a room-by-room inventory of what you're keeping, donating, or discarding
- Request your security deposit itemization policy from your new landlord in writing

## 2 weeks before moving
- Schedule utility transfers (electric, gas, internet) to start the day you get keys
- Submit a change-of-address with USPS (takes effect in 7-10 business days)
- Confirm renters insurance is active before move-in day (most landlords require proof)

## 1 week before moving
- Confirm delivery windows with movers and building management (many buildings require a reserved elevator slot)
- Pack an "open first" box: toiletries, chargers, basic tools, a shower curtain
- Do a final walkthrough of your old place for damage documentation

## Move-in day
- Photograph every room, closet, and appliance before you unpack anything — this is your move-in condition record
- Test smoke detectors, all outlets, and water pressure within the first 24 hours
- Locate the water shutoff valve and breaker panel

## What documents do I need to bring when moving into a new apartment?
Government-issued photo ID, signed lease copy, proof of renters insurance, and your security deposit receipt. Bring these physically — don't rely on email access during the move.

## How early should I set up utilities before moving in?
Schedule transfers 10-14 days ahead. Most providers need 5-7 business days minimum, and same-day setup is rarely available in high-demand buildings.

## What should I photograph before moving into a new apartment?
Every wall, floor, and fixture — including existing scuffs, stains, or damage — before a single box goes down. This record is what protects your deposit at move-out.

*Sources: USPS mail forwarding processing times; standard U.S. residential lease and security-deposit practices.*
